# The PassVault Licence

Plain English on purpose. If a term here is ever unclear, the reading that favours you is
the correct one.

> **Not legal advice.** This was written to be honest and readable, not to be bulletproof in
> court. If you start selling PassVault properly, have a lawyer read it.

---

## What you get

When you buy PassVault, you buy it. Not a subscription, not a seat, not permission that
someone can take back.

**It never expires.** There is no renewal date. There is nothing to re-buy.

**There is no account.** PassVault has no sign-up, no login and no profile. Nothing to lose
access to.

**It never phones home.** PassVault does not contact any server — not to check your licence,
not to count you, not for anything. It works with the network cable pulled out, forever.

**Nobody can switch it off.** Your licence is checked on your own machine against a key built
into the app. There is no server that could go down, no list you could be removed from, and no
switch anybody can flip — including me.

**Your data is yours, unconditionally.** Your vault is a file on your stick and on your PC. It
is encrypted with keys only you hold. Even with physical access to it, I could not open it.
Nothing in this licence gives me any claim over it.

**You can use it on every machine you personally use.** Your desktop, your laptop, the PC at
your parents' house. One person, as many machines as that person uses.

## If this project is abandoned

**If there is no new release of PassVault for 24 months, the full source code will be
published under an open licence.**

This is the part that matters most, and it is a promise, not a plan.

Software you depend on can die when its author loses interest, changes job, or stops caring.
For a password manager that is not an inconvenience — it is your entire digital life sitting
in a format nobody can maintain. This clause means that cannot happen to you. If PassVault
stops being looked after, it becomes something anybody can look after.

The file format is already written down in full, in `DESIGN.md`, with test vectors. Even today,
before any of that clause matters, somebody could write their own reader for your vault.

## The free version

**The free version of PassVault is complete and safe. It always will be.**

Specifically, these are never behind a payment, in any version, ever:

- Storing as many credentials as you like
- The full strength of the encryption — the same AES-256-GCM and the same Argon2id settings
- Your 12-word recovery phrase
- Backing up your vault
- Re-keying after a stick is lost or stolen
- Importing from other password managers and browsers
- Getting your data back out

Paying is for things that save you **time**. It is never for things that keep you **safe**.
Charging someone for their own security is not a business model I want.

The free version will remind you, politely, that it exists. It will never block you, never
count down at you, and never make itself worse to push you into paying.

## What buying gets you

Convenience, and supporting the work:

- Your name in the app, because you own it
- Auto-type, when it exists - one key types your login into any window
- More than one vault, so work and personal stay apart, when it exists
- The browser extension, when it exists
- Sync between your devices, when it exists

Importing used to be on this list. It moved to the free list before anyone paid for it:
getting your passwords *in* is how you switch to PassVault, and charging for the way in is
the wrong way round.

## What you cannot do

Short list, and none of it will surprise you:

- Do not sell PassVault, or a modified version of it, as your own
- Do not share your licence key publicly

Your key is not copy-protected in any serious way, and that is deliberate. It is checked
offline, on your own machine, and a determined person could get around it in an afternoon.
The lock on your front door works the same way. It is there to be clear about what is yours,
not to fight you.

Honesty matters more here than in most software, so:

**No warranty.** PassVault is provided as is. I have built it carefully, tested it, and had
the design attacked from several angles — but it has not had an independent security audit,
and I am one person.

**I am not liable** if you lose data or if something goes wrong. Read `DESIGN.md`, which sets
out plainly what PassVault protects you from and what it does not.

**Back up your vault, and write your 12 words on paper.** The app makes both easy. No licence
term can substitute for either.
